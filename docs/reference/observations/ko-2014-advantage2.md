# Advantage, Efficiency, and Game Theory (Part 2 of 3)

The second of three posts by Chris Hodges of Des Moines Kubb, dated April 28, 2014, from
the Des Moines Kubb blog, in Kubb On's archive. It proposes measuring play by efficiency
rather than accuracy, and gives a table of "Baton Value": the average number of batons it
takes to clear a position, by field kubbs, base kubbs and whether there is an advantage
line. His data: the 100-odd games recorded throw by throw (Planet Kubb), about 900 turns,
plenty of turns with 2 to 8 field kubbs and few with 1, 9 or 10, which he fills by
regression. He shows two recorded games in abbreviated Planet Kubb Notation, without
naming the teams or the event, and names one team: Kubb'Ings, then world champions.

Reading the notation as given: each line is a turn, the first item the kubbs thrown in
(`3I`) and the rest one baton each, `-` a miss, `B` a base kubb, `nF` n field kubbs, `K`
the king. Odd turns are Team A, even turns Team B.
