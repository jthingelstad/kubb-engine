# The Drill Interviews

"The Drill: The Most Important Throw In Kubb - the interviews...in entirety", Kubbnation
Magazine, January 2012, volume 2. Page 1 is a cover with a map pointing to where each of
the fifteen interviewees lives. Pages 2 to 6 print their answers about the drilled
inkast, one heading per player ("Name - Place"), with the interviewer's questions in bold.
The questions differ from player to player. The editor's introduction says the words are
the players' own, kept as written; it also says the piece grew to seven pages, though the
file has six. The players speak about the 2011 season and earlier.

Nine answers run over from the foot of one column to the top of the next, where no
heading repeats the name: Niklas Ahlgren (top of page 2, right), Terry Ekelöf (page 3,
left), Josh Feathers (page 3, right), Eric Goplin (page 4, left), Björn Görlitz (page 4,
right), Marcus Hansson (page 5, left), Jens Haraldsson (page 5, right), Johan Lundin
(page 6, left) and Simon Pettersson (page 6, right). Most of these split a sentence or a
question across the break, which settles the speaker. Three do not: Niklas's (a new
paragraph on the same subject, the 2011 World Championship), Björn's and Marcus's (new
questions); there the speaker is the only one whose section the column continues, and
the content agrees (Björn's "see the first sentences", Marcus's barbecue and Team Ekeby).
Simon's (page 6, right) splits nothing either: it is named Simon because his section ends
the left column and Sören Wallin's heading comes after it.

## The fifteen

As the piece gives them, in its order. Place is from each heading; anything else is what
the player or another interviewee says in the piece.

- **Niklas Ahlgren**, Katthammarsvik, Sweden. Inkastare of Elefantbajs 2000 (first called
  Team Spendrups) since 1999 or 2000; the team's World Championship bronze 2004, silver
  2005, and the 2006 final won against Team Ekeby. Sören Wallin calls him "Wizzard of
  Drill 2011".
- **Josef Björklund**, Hemse, Sweden. Aged 22, from Rone on Gotland; the "kubbas" of
  Menage à trois. In Sören Wallin's 2011 top four and Johan Lundin's top five.
- **Joakim Ekelöf**, Landskrona, Sweden. No team in his heading; he speaks of how Team
  Ekeby throws.
- **Terry Ekelöf**, Stockholm, Sweden. No team given.
- **Josh Feathers**, Des Moines, Iowa. Was preparing for the 2010 U.S. Nationals when he
  first saw drilling. No team given.
- **Scott Forster**, Eau Claire, Wisconsin. No team given.
- **Eric Goplin**, Minneapolis, Minnesota. No team given.
- **Björn Görlitz**, Berlin, Germany. Inkastare of Fortschritt99 for the last four years;
  the team's results 2008 second, 2009 third, 2010 third, 2011 European champion and a
  2011 World Championship quarter-final. Sören Wallin calls him "Master of the Margins".
- **Marcus Hansson**, Visby, Sweden. Team Ekeby's inkastare at the 2011 Kubb VM.
- **Jens Haraldsson**, Stockholm, Sweden. No team given.
- **Samuel Hufschmid**, Basel, Switzerland. Inkastare of Team SMP Basel United; in
  tournaments since 2009.
- **Johan Lundin**, Visby, Sweden. No team given; rates himself top 3 on a good day and
  top 10 to 15 overall.
- **Paul Mullen**, River Falls, Wisconsin. No team given.
- **Simon Pettersson**, Hemse, Sweden. Playing since about age 6, about 12 years. In Sören
  Wallin's 2011 top four.
- **Sören Wallin**, Stockholm, Sweden. No team given; credited with the photographs on
  page 6.

## Questions for Jamie

1. **Still 2011?** These answers predate rule changes Björn Görlitz and Samuel Hufschmid
   asked for (a longer pitch with 12 kubbs; one try per kubb instead of two). Have the
   rules for the inkast changed since, and do any of these habits (aiming out on
   purpose, `p5.15`; taking the rethrow over a bad kubb, `p3.10`) still hold under the
   rules the game uses?
2. **Raising on the line.** Niklas Ahlgren (`p2.2`) says World Championship rules raise a
   kubb on the line inwards; Josh Feathers (`p3.7`) and Simon Pettersson (`p6.8`) give
   the same reason for the lines. Is that still the rule, and is it the engine's rule?
3. **Depth or closeness.** Josh Feathers (`p3.13`) and Jens Haraldsson (`p5.13`) value
   depth over packing every kubb tight; Simon Pettersson (`p6.9`) and Josef Björklund
   (`p2.10`) aim for kubbs so close the raiser has no choice. Are these the same goal
   in different words, or a real disagreement?
4. **Where the group goes.** Most aim for the corner of centre line and sideline; Josh
   Feathers puts a single kubb midway between the king and the centre-line pin
   (`p3.11`) and starts a big pile on the centre line by the pin (`p3.12`), and Simon
   Pettersson names the "middle left or right side pin" (`p6.8`). Are these the same
   spot? Which pin is the middle side pin?
5. **Deep kubbs.** Joakim Ekelöf (`p2.16`, `p2.17`) sends the kubbs his team cannot clear
   to the base line in a cluster, and says Team Ekeby normally throws everything short
   (`p2.18`). Is the base-line cluster the same play as PLAY.md's deep kubbs at 7 or 8
   m, and is it still a weaker team's tool?
6. **Bounce or land.** Terry Ekelöf (`p3.1`) and Eric Goplin (`p4.2`) prefer a kubb that
   lands where it is meant to and stays; Josef Björklund, Scott Forster, Marcus Hansson
   and Samuel Hufschmid bounce or roll each kubb against the ones already down. Which is
   the pro inkast today, or does it depend on the ground, as Johan Lundin says
   (`p6.2`)?
7. **Steering the roll.** Joakim Ekelöf steers by angling the back end of the kubb
   (`p2.13`), Jens Haraldsson by grip and a held wrist tilt (`p5.9`), Marcus Hansson by
   the hand's angle (`p5.1`). Are these one technique described three ways?
8. **What Johan Lundin means** in `p6.5`: knocking a kubb that is "not over line" so both
   end over it. Is that a kubb short of the centre line, one outside a sideline, or a
   standing field kubb (PLAY.md's rescue)?
9. **How often the best err.** Niklas Ahlgren made about ten mistakes over a World
   Championship and counted one by Fortschritt 99's inkastare as decisive (`p2.6`,
   `p2.7`). Does that match what a top inkastare's miss rate looks like now?
10. **Rethrows as a free look.** Josh Feathers and Samuel Hufschmid both say a kubb that
    goes out is better than a bad one that stays in (`p3.10`, `p5.15`). Is that the
    deliberate risk PLAY.md's open question 1 asks about?
