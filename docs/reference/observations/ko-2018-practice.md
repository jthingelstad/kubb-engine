# How I Practice: Kyle Weakland

"How I Practice: Kyle Weakland", Kubb On, by Kyle Weakland, dated July 23, 2018. He
describes the practice games he plays alone, with his own targets. The author's note says
he met kubb in 2013, has played competitively since 2015, and co-founded the Burning River
Kubb Club and Kubb On. Two comments follow: one from a commenter named only "el ringer",
who describes a drill he credits to "Björn", and Kyle's reply. The drill he adapts is
credited to Eric Anderson of Wisconsin Kubb.

## Entries

| `p1.1` | Kyle Weakland | practice | He has no other players nearby and practises alone; to improve he makes practice harder than a game, since phantom games against himself only take him so far. | said |
| `p1.2` | Kyle Weakland | practice | His inkast game is Eric Anderson's 3-4-3 (inkasting three or four kubbs into a pile), made harder. | said |
| `p1.3` | Kyle Weakland | field | Groups of three or four are seen in a match more often than eight, nine or ten (no count given). Taking three or four kubbs with one or two batons early in a match saves batons that can decide it. | said |
| `p1.4` | Kyle Weakland | practice | His version: inkast groups of three, four and three into open ground instead of at a corner pin, which is much harder because no sideline forces the kubbs together. | said |
| `p1.5` | Kyle Weakland | practice | He then blasts the three groups with six batons, aiming to clear all three. His further goal, met once, is to clear them with five batons and topple the king with the sixth. His own count. | said |
| `p1.6` | Kyle Weakland | baseline | He has no special drills for 8 m; controlling the baton's rotation is what matters most there. | said |
| `p1.7` | Kyle Weakland | baseline | In practice he almost never throws at a kubb from 8 m but at a corner pin, which is narrower. Two or three hits on a corner pin from six throws he counts as good. His own benchmark; no record given. | said |
| `p1.8` | Kyle Weakland | practice | For general accuracy he sets six corner pins at different distances, one at 4 m, one at 8 m and four between, and tries to hit each once with his six batons. This trains the 5 to 7 m range that otherwise goes unpractised. | said |
| `p1.9` | Kyle Weakland | practice | His all-round game: inkast all 10 kubbs as in a match and raise them to make the group as hard as he can. | said |
| `p1.10` | Kyle Weakland | punish | When making the group hard, he puts penalty kubbs at about 7 m and exact: "neighbor kubbs" somewhere in the group. Neighbor kubbs are not explained. | said |
| `p1.11` | Kyle Weakland | practice | With six batons he must clear the 10 field kubbs, hit the corner pin and topple the king. His usual goal is to clear the field in three batons, leaving two for the 8 m pin and one for the king; the further goal, clearing everything in five or fewer, he has met a few times. His own count. | said |
| `p1.12` | el ringer (comment) | practice | Björn showed them an accuracy game: golf balls on tees where the kubbs and king stand; driving a ball straight back between the baseline stakes earns a free baton, and toppled balls are inkasted and teed up. The commenter adds that he and Sy beat Björn and Big G the first time they met. | said |
| `p1.13` | Kyle Weakland (comment) | practice | He likes the idea; he looks for training harder than the game that still feels like the game. | said |
