# Kubb Rescue Squad

"Kubb Rescue Squad", by Josh Feathers, dated March 16, 2012, filed under Strategy on Kubb
On and marked as reposted from the Des Moines Kubb Blog Archive. It explains the rescue:
throwing a kubb in hand at a field kubb standing from an earlier round so that both go
out of bounds and come back to be thrown again. It rests on one rule, which it quotes
(U.S. National Kubb Championship Rules, Sec. II.C.5, linked as the v2 PDF on
desmoineskubb.com), and on the author's own reasoning; he says he has never seen the play
tried in a tournament. The Planet Kubb wiki's Strategy page (`pk-strategy`) has a rescue
section in the same terms, last modified three days later.

| `p1.4` | Josh Feathers | rules | He quotes U.S. National Championship Rules Sec. II.C.5: when a thrown field kubb strikes a field kubb in play and the struck kubb comes to rest where it cannot be raised in bounds, the struck kubb goes back to the attacking team to be thrown, as though not yet thrown this round. The rule applies only to field kubbs left standing in a previous round. (The quotation has a clause "(a)" and no "(b)".) | said |
| `p1.5` | Josh Feathers | rules | His reading: you may use a kubb in hand to knock a field kubb from an earlier round out of bounds, rescuing it from a bad position. | said |
| `p1.6` | Josh Feathers | inkast | The payoff is greatest in a close game or with many kubbs in play: the rescued kubb and the one thrown at it are thrown again near the main pile, raising the chance of doubles and triples. | said |
| `p1.7` | Josh Feathers | inkast | The risks: a light touch may roll or tip the struck kubb into a worse place; if the thrown kubb stays in bounds, you now have two kubbs in bad places. | said |
| `p1.8` | Josh Feathers | inkast | When: he advises a rescue only when the established kubb is very close to a sideline or on the far baseline. | said |
| `p1.9` | Josh Feathers | inkast | Do not rescue for its own sake: be confident that success will swing the game, or let you win it in that round. The game has so many factors that the best moment is hard to pick. | said |
| `p1.10` | Josh Feathers | inkast | You can also rescue kubbs you have just thrown, if each was thrown only once, but these are normally lying down, and rescuing a kubb that is not upright is not worth it in almost any case. How this fits the quoted rule's limit to kubbs left standing from a previous round (`p1.4`) is not explained. | said |
| `p1.11` | Josh Feathers | inkast | Angle of attack matters most. From a sideline, take a heavy angle, so the line of the throw drives both kubbs out. Knocking one out while the thrown kubb stays in gains nothing. | said |
| `p1.12` | Josh Feathers | inkast | One throw: hold the kubb in the middle and throw it with its long side facing the target (a exact: "bowling throw"). The wider face is easier to hit with, and a kubb thrown so is likelier to roll out. | said |
| `p1.13` | Josh Feathers | inkast | Aim so the thrown kubb does not touch the grass before it strikes: a bounce first can send it off line or over the target. | said |
| `p1.14` | Josh Feathers | inkast | The other throw: a "helicopter", holding one end and spinning the kubb through the throw. It rolls less than the bowling throw but carries a little more energy to push both out. | said |
| `p1.15` | Josh Feathers | rules | Kubbs may be thrown with any underarm motion, so the helicopter throw cannot be called an illegal propeller throw (that limit is for batons, by implication). | said |
| `p1.16` | Josh Feathers | rules | Custom, not rule: tell the opponents and any spectators before a rescue, so they can protect their shins; a kubb hurts more than a baton. | said |
